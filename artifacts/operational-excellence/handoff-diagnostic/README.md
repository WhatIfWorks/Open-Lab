# Handoff Diagnostic

**Historical artifact identifier:** Open Lab #001

The Handoff Diagnostic is a simple practitioner tool for examining what happens **between** processes, functions, teams, or individuals.

It started with a basic observation:

> A process can look healthy from inside each function while the system still performs poorly at the handoffs between them.

## Purpose

Examine what happens between processes, functions, teams, or individuals. Make the interface visible so you can better understand where work, information, responsibility, authority, or decisions succeed or break down.

## What Problem Is It Trying to Solve?

Organizations often investigate performance by looking at individual processes or functions:

- Did Team A follow its process?
- Did Team B meet its target?
- Was the procedure followed?
- Did each department complete its responsibilities?

Those are useful questions.

But they can miss a different class of failure:

**What happened between A and B?**

Information may be incomplete.

Ownership may become ambiguous.

Timing expectations may differ.

One team may believe the work is finished while the next believes it hasn't properly begun.

The individual processes can appear functional while the overall system struggles.

## Intended Audience

Anyone investigating work that crosses boundaries, including practitioners in:

- Quality;
- Continuous Improvement;
- Operational Excellence;
- process improvement;
- operations;
- project or program management;
- investigations and root cause analysis;
- organizational design;
- cross-functional leadership.

The model is intentionally industry-agnostic.

Different environments will require different questions, terminology, controls, and levels of rigor.

## When to Use It

Use it when work crosses a boundary and local process performance does not explain the system's results, or when transfer, ownership, readiness, exceptions, or feedback are unclear.

## How to Use It

Start with a real workflow that crosses at least one boundary.

Identify the handoff.

Then examine the interface rather than immediately diagnosing either side.

Use the worksheet's Handoff Map to identify the sending side, receiving side, transfer, mechanism/channel, trigger, and expected next activity. Capture each side's description independently in “Sending side says...” and “Receiving side says...” before comparing their accounts.

Explore all nine lenses: Transfer, Mechanism, Ownership, Information, Expectations, Timing, Acceptance, Exceptions, and Feedback. Synthesize agreements, differences, visible assumptions, deeper investigation, evidence needs, and what should not yet be assumed.

The diagnostic provides questions intended to help explore:

- what is being transferred;
- who owns what;
- what information travels with the work;
- how readiness is determined;
- what each side expects;
- how exceptions are handled;
- how feedback travels back across the boundary.

You do not need to use every question.

You probably shouldn't.

Use what helps you understand the problem.

Change what doesn't.

## What Assumptions Does It Make?

The diagnostic assumes that:

- work crosses identifiable boundaries;
- both sides of the handoff can provide useful information;
- local performance does not necessarily describe system performance;
- interface failures may exist even when individual processes appear compliant or effective;
- the purpose of the diagnostic is understanding, not assigning blame.

## Limitations

This is not:

- a validated assessment;
- a regulatory standard;
- a maturity model;
- a substitute for process mapping;
- a root cause analysis method;
- a scoring system;
- proof that a handoff is the cause of a problem.

It is a way to ask better questions about an often-overlooked part of a system.

## What Can I Change?

Anything.

Add questions.

Remove questions.

Adapt it for your industry.

Turn it into a workshop.

If you find a better way to examine handoffs, we'd like to see it.

## Where Did This Come From?

This artifact grew from a broader observation about organizational systems:

**Processes do not operate in isolation.**

The interfaces between them can create failure modes that remain difficult to see when each function is evaluated independently.

Open Lab #001 is an attempt to turn that observation into something practitioners can test against real work.

## Expected Outputs

- A handoff map showing the boundary, transfer, channel, trigger, and next activity.
- Independent sending-side and receiving-side descriptions.
- Observations and evidence across the nine lenses.
- A synthesis of agreements, differences, assumptions, further investigation, evidence needs, and unresolved conclusions.
