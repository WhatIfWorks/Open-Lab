# Alignment vs. Standardization Diagnostic

## Purpose

Determine where consistency is necessary, where alignment is sufficient, and where local variation can legitimately remain.

## Intended Audience

Quality, Continuous Improvement, Operational Excellence, process owners, and cross-functional or multi-site teams deciding what must be shared and what may vary.

## When to Use It

Use it when differences between teams, sites, or methods prompt a proposal to standardize, or when operating boundaries and shared requirements need clarification.

## How to Use It

1. Establish the outcome and where variation creates meaningful risk or incompatibility.
2. Examine Outcome, Requirement, Interface, Evidence, Variation, Consequence, Local Value, and Standardization; record observations and evidence.
3. Test whether variation creates a real problem and whether the method itself must match. Consider standardizing the method, aligning requirements/interfaces/evidence/outcomes, or allowing legitimate variation.
4. Define what must be common, what must be identical, what may vary, and how those boundaries will remain governed.

## Expected Outputs

- Evidence about actual variation, its consequences, and any local value it preserves.
- A reasoned distinction between alignment, standardization, and legitimate variation.
- An operating boundary covering Align, Standardize, Allow Variation, and Govern.

## Limitations

This is not an argument against standards, a mandate for local autonomy, or a scoring system for process consistency. Non-negotiable requirements remain applicable. Variation is not automatically a defect, and consistency is not automatically evidence of control.
