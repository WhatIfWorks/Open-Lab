# Control vs. Capability Diagnostic

## Purpose

Examine whether a process can reliably produce the required outcome—or whether controls are compensating for weaknesses in the process itself.

## Intended Audience

Quality, Continuous Improvement, Operational Excellence, operations, and process owners examining process performance and the controls surrounding it.

## When to Use It

Use it when assessing reliance on inspection, review, monitoring, or intervention, or deciding where improvement effort belongs between process capability and surrounding controls.

## How to Use It

1. Define the required outcome, evidence of capability, and controls used to keep performance acceptable.
2. Examine Outcome, Method, Variation, Failure, Prevention, Detection, Intervention, and Dependency; record observations and evidence.
3. Test a control's function, reason, and what would happen without it. This is an investigative thought exercise, not an instruction to remove the control.
4. Consider retaining controls for legitimate requirements or residual risk, building prevention/control into the process, or reducing dependency through improved capability.
5. Describe Capability, Variation, Control, and Evidence in the operating state.

## Expected Outputs

- A description of required performance, process variation/failure, and current controls.
- A dependency test identifying a control's function, necessity, and consequences of its absence.
- An operating-state description of capability, remaining variation, necessary controls, and evidence of continued performance.

## Limitations

This is not an argument against controls, a control-risk assessment, a statistical capability analysis, or a reason to remove required safeguards. Preserve controls that address legitimate requirements or residual risk. The diagnostic offers qualitative investigation rather than a capability statistic or score.
